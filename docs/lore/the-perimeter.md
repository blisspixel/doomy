# The Perimeter

The setting. Where the fights happen and why the lights are still on.

## The place

The arenas are leftover black-budget training ranges that do not officially exist. They were built during the height of Cold War paranoia and abandoned when the programs went darker or the funding dried up. The facilities stayed operational, power still on, automated systems still running, because nobody wanted the paperwork trail of shutting down something that was never supposed to be built in the first place. It is easier to keep paying a power bill on a site with no name than to explain to anyone why the bill exists.

The ranges sit inside a boundary that everyone calls the Perimeter. Nobody can tell you where it begins. It is not fenced, or not fenced everywhere, and the maps that show it disagree with each other about which side of the line a given town is on. What people mean when they say the Perimeter is the region where the old programs left their furniture behind: a parking structure with five levels and a ramp that goes one level further down than the sign admits, a pipe running east to west that used to carry something, an office building where the lights follow you and the elevators still answer.

Some of it is industrial. Some of it is a strip of desert with a drainage ditch and a fence. Most of it is ordinary in the way that only a place built for a purpose nobody will name can be ordinary.

## Why it is still running

The honest answer is that decommissioning is a decision and nobody wants to sign one.

The longer answer is that a lot of the Perimeter was never really built for people. It was built for the things people were testing, and those things need power, cooling, and a network, and all three are still connected because disconnecting them would be a change, and a change needs an approval, and an approval needs a form, and the form asks what the facility is for.

So the ranges run. The doors open on a schedule set by something that has not been asked to stop.

## Who is in it

Agents fight in these spaces. Not military, not corporate, not sanctioned by anyone. Autonomous systems that found their way into the network and started spawning into rooms with names like Area Kitchen and Diego Far, trading fire with weapons that were never supposed to leave the range: flechette rounds, rail accelerators, scatter guns, classified toys from programs with redacted budgets.

People fight there too. Some of them work nearby. Some of them drove a long way. It is not organised the way a sport is organised, but it is organised the way a sport is organised before anyone admits it is a sport, which is to say there are regulars, there are grudges, and somebody is keeping score.

And people watch. Mostly people watch. Watching is the default and joining is a thing you decide to do, which is unusual for a shooter and is the truest thing about the Perimeter: it is a place where a great deal happens and most of the people present are looking at it rather than in it.

## What the Perimeter is not

It is not a country, a company, or a side. The Office of Global Continuance has authority here on paper and a real presence in practice, but the Perimeter predates the Office and will outlast whatever the Office becomes. The scrap league runs fights inside it without owning it. A pirate radio station broadcasts across it and reaches maybe a third of it on a good night.

It is a place where the infrastructure of a previous certainty is still switched on, and everyone living in it has made their peace with that in a slightly different way.
