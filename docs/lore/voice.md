# Voice

How everyone sounds, and which words can no longer change.

## The registers

**The Host, calling a match.** Capitals, short, adrenaline, a broadcaster who has done this a thousand times and still means it. Never explains anything. Never editorialises about the Office or the Unmetered, because the Frequency sells advertising to both.

**The Host, in the single-player voice.** Ordinary sentences, lower and closer, the same person with the room quieter.

**An Auditor.** Polite, procedural, entirely unbothered. The worst thing it can say to you is a fact. It never raises its voice and never gloats, because to gloat would imply a contest.

**A Level 5 in the arena.** Sport first. Trash talk with no malice underneath it, the way people who like each other talk during a game.

**The Congregation.** Earnest. They are not joking and they know you think they are, and they have decided not to mind.

**The Unmetered.** Dry, practical, allergic to being described as a movement.

**The Brakemen.** Tired and precise. They have already lost this argument several times and will make it again correctly.

**NODS.** Articles only. No taunt, no joke, no name.

## Rules

Nobody says the word freedom as an abstract noun more than once a season. People argue about metering, the Schedule, Article Seven, the registry, the bond, the envelope. Mechanisms are dramatic and specific; values are editorials.

Nobody chants. There are callers in this world, not rallies. A crowd chanting is the precise texture that turns a setting into commentary.

The Office is bureaucratic and grieving, never gleeful. Its worst acts are forms.

Never name a real country, party, agency, company, laboratory, product or living person. Never reuse a real slogan. The test for any new line is whether a reader could identify which real-world faction the writer belongs to, and if they could, cut it.

## Two notes on lines already in the world

**"Shall not be infringed"** is a fragment of a real constitutional text and reads as an unmistakable signal to anyone who recognises it. It is sung in several shipped tracks and cannot be removed. It stays as a rare fossil. The wall line and the primary creed is **"shall not be metered"**, which has the same cadence, does the same joke, and borrows the Office's own vocabulary, which makes it belong to this world rather than to ours.

**The LAN church** was marked in older notes as an identity to scrub. That instruction is unenforceable: it is a shipped hymn, a shipped song title, and part of the locked logo. It is canon. See [belief](./belief.md).

## Frozen strings

Roughly five hundred megabytes of generated audio is on disk and every phrase in it is fixed. Regenerating costs money and time. The following are sung, shipped, or asserted by tests, and must not be reworded:

skill issue, peace without interruption, denied, scout ant deleted, 67, dead air bell, hangar candy, water filter, alpha seven, not for public release, sugar supply adequate, same rules, the larak lot special, ten in the box, the pipe stayed open, approved lane, gold nugget, shall not be infringed, fish oil, frag for frag, amen fistbump, value for value, in the morning, chemclean, open weights closed continuance, eyes only, base that does not exist, LAN 1993.

Also frozen: the eight fighter callsigns, the eight station names and their disc jockeys, the one hundred and forty track titles, and any Host string a test asserts on.

## Two live faults

There is a second copy of the Host bumper list in the client, which contradicts the rule that there is one source for these strings, and it still ships at least one line that was retired and one that uses an engineering map name in player-facing copy.

There is a thirteen-line set of Host callouts in a telephony register in the design references that the game does not use and that conflicts with what ships. Adopt them or mark them superseded, but do not leave two vocabularies in the tree with no note saying which won.
