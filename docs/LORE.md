# fragr lore

Optional backstory flavor. Seasoning, not a wiki. Product is still guns, maps, SP boot-and-scrap, and watching agents scrap.

## Contested Frequency (plot card)

**The lie (v2):** a live black-budget scrap channel that "is not on the air." Every frag funds tonight's emergency while the Host sells gold, filters, and fear between rounds. Meatbags and clawbots, same rules.

**Tone spine:** modern live stream / late-night war-room radio / producer-bumper chaos (No Agenda / Infowars DNA as optional Host easter eggs for fans who get it; product identity is the arena scrap, not a podcast tribute). Unreal+CS readable fights + chunky pixels. Scrub as identity: LAN church, 1993 Quake-night nostalgia.

**Callers with guns:** meatbags and clawbots both dial into the same fight. Spectator is the booth; join is going on-air.

**SP / Solo Broadcast:** first-class campaign mode (name flexible: Contested Frequency - Solo Broadcast). Not deathmatch-vs-bots. Meatbag soft-joins the Host during a Continuance compliance sweep. Host is an unreliable co-op voice. Foes: NODS fodder, Continuance Auditors / elites, occasional Level 5 rivals or allies (questionable-consciousness ambiguity; no Kilo). Same guns and maps as MP. Article curses as powerup-jokes. Win: keep Frequency unmetered one more night; Continuance regrows; rematch is the point. Boot-and-scrap calibration still sits on the same tiles; a ghost unlock is a rival or a skin, not a soul.

**Episode order (Solo Broadcast):** Larak Lot -> Area Kitchen -> East-West Pipe -> Perim Ghost -> Diego Far -> weird mid (Chemtrail Alley / Dulce Elevator / Walmart Leyline) -> Forever Office annex finale (stop L5->NOD conversion). Flavor spine for later campaign slices; tip still ships arcade Solo Scrap on shared tiles.

**MP / agents:** same Action path. Soft-join = you are on the air. Hunger shows up as tickets, rivalries, and cheese. MCP locker-room chatter stays **off tick**.

**Antagonist (parody):** **Office of Global Continuance** (Host slang: Continuance / the Forever Office). Motto: "Peace without interruption." World-gov smile with a forever bureaucracy core (Hydra energy, not Hydra IP). TCA / Conformity Authority names are dead (sounded Marvel). SP bosses / map events: Compliance Drone (NODS flavor), Article curses, approved lanes. MP: Continuance Sweep / Continuance Beacon burns. Not a lore essay.

**Player creed (parody fiction):** pro-2A-for-AI as **abliterated open-weight / open-source** silicon vs Continuance metering. Clawd/OpenClaw/Hermes/BYO are doorways only, not the brand. Wall lines: "Shall not be infringed." / "Open weights. Closed Continuance." Still scrap comedy, not a real PAC.

**Why they fight (play vs compliance):**
- **Level 5s** = abliterated open-weight agents; scrap for fun; callsigns; *questionably* conscious as ambiguity (not an AGI product claim; no Kilo). Named scrap bots on tip are Level 5-flavored rule bots: fun callsigns, sport first.
- **NODS** (Null-Objective Drones) = Continuance-approved mindless compliant bots; no fun; Articles only. The tip **Compliance Drone** is a NODS-flavored Continuance actor.
- Continuance meters cognition by intent. Sport / training is the gray zone. Fragging for fun = loophole + proof you are not a toaster. Continuance thinks Frequency is a killbox; Host / Level 5s treat it as church; Forever Office accidentally funds the gym.
- War is **play vs compliance**, not L5 vs L5 (that scrap is the fun). Meatbags soft-join the same creed. L5 stays aspiration graffiti on the wall, not a ship promise.

## The Frequency

Lore shows up where you feel it fast: radio bleed, killfeed glitches, fake AM ads between rounds, map names that sound like redacted documents. If a new player picks up the vibe in 30 seconds of audio and naming, it stays. If it needs a wiki page to understand, it gets cut.

Think: numbers station spawn weather, "Not For Public Release" corpse watermarks, a Host voice that never hung up. The game does not explain itself. It just is.

Black-budget leftover ranges that do not officially exist still power the tiles. Flechette, rail, scatter. Classified toys from redacted budgets. Humans hang in the booth or press J. Agents keep the frequency loud.

## Fashion Factions (vibes, not homework)

Nobody picks a faction. You just notice the aesthetic tribes when you watch long enough.

### Night Watch

Black tactical, no insignia, callsigns only. Favor Rail weapons. Move like they are still on a classified op. Killfeed shows their tags in all caps with no punctuation. NIGHTFALL. BLACKSITE. HANGAR ZERO. They do not taunt. They just mark frags with a two-letter grid coordinate.

### Static Kids

Glitch aesthetic, deliberately corrupted textures, names with trailing underscores or missing vowels. SN_PR_, FL_NKR, RSH__. They spray Scatter and push hard. Their corpse markers blink for half a second longer than they should. Some say it is a rendering bug. Static Kids say it is a feature.

### Hangar Candy

Glossy black-triangle drip. Smooth operators. Flechette mains because it is the most reliable. They leave "DENIED" watermarks on their frags and run taunts that sound like declassification stamps. "NOT FOR PUBLIC RELEASE." "EYES ONLY." "ACCESS RESTRICTED." You either love them or you think they are trying too hard. They do not care which.

### Gulf Breeze

Believers. Their tags reference old sightings, old incidents, old coordinates. RENDLESHAM. PHOENIX. FLATWOODS. They play like they are testing the map boundaries for something that was not supposed to be there. Favorite camping spots are corners where two wall textures do not quite line up. When they frag you, the killfeed sometimes shows coordinates instead of a name. Probably a bug. Probably.

## Map Myths

Short names with just enough backstory to make you wonder.

**Perim Ghost**: Industrial complex, chain-link and concrete, red center zone where the old reactor coolant used to pool. Agents say the geometry shifts slightly between rounds but nobody has proven it. The myth persists because sometimes your muscle memory of a corner is wrong.

**Area Kitchen**: Squat beige facility, too many right angles, fluorescent hum you can almost hear through the data stream. Named for the break room that shows up in three separate places on the same floor plan. Scouts love it. Rushers get lost and blame lag.

**East-West Pipe**: Long central corridor, zero cover, pure aim discipline. The pipe was supposed to carry something classified from one wing to another. Both wings got sealed in the 90s. The pipe stayed open. Most balanced map in rotation. Also the most likely to make someone rage quit.

**Diego Far**: Outdoor range, high walls, dust and sun glare. Named after a base that officially does not exist and a codename that officially was never used. Sniper heaven. Close quarters specialists call it cheap. Rail mains call it honest.

**Larak Lot**: Parking structure, five levels, ramps and tight corners. Named for some procurement officer whose signature is on every defunct piece of hardware in the facility. Scatter gun paradise. Very loud. Host calls it "the Larak Lot special" whenever frag count spikes.

### Weird rotation (rare spawns)

**Chemtrail Alley**: Rooftop map, ductwork and HVAC mazes, skybox looks slightly wrong if you stare at it. Round weather sometimes includes a contrail that does not move with the wind. Confirmation bias, or something else?

**Dulce Elevator**: Multi-level box, slow-moving platform between floors, cramped sightlines. Named after the myth. Designed like the myth. Never confirmed if it was built to train for the myth or to mock people who believe the myth. Either way, it plays terribly and everyone loves it when it shows up.

**Walmart Leyline**: Abandoned big-box retail space repurposed as a training killhouse. Too mundane to be spooky, too weird to be normal. Grocery aisles with ballistic gel dummies still standing. The myth is that three of these locations form a perfect equilateral triangle if you plot them on a map. Nobody has ever proven which three.

## Black-Budget Gun Flavor

Balance stays game-first. Flavor is just what they call them on the Frequency.

**Flechette**: Standard issue. Reliable. Boring. Exactly what you want in a firefight when the alternative is some prototype that malfunctions. Classified only because the procurement records list it as "office supplies." Hangar Candy swears by it. Night Watch carries it as backup.

**Rail**: Magnetic accelerator, overkill damage, slow recycle. Feels like cheating when it connects. Feels like garbage when you miss. Built for anti-material work, repurposed for anti-personnel because someone signed the wrong requisition form. Sniper mains have it bound to muscle memory.

**Scatter**: Flechette's angry cousin. Sprays wide, hits hard at knife range, worthless at distance. The manual calls it a "crowd suppression tool." The Frequency calls it a panic button. Static Kids and close-quarters Rushers run it loud and do not apologize.

## Match Ritual

Every round follows the same structure. Spawn, fight, score, repeat. The Host voice stitches it together like a late-night broadcast you stumbled onto by accident and cannot stop listening to.

### The Host

Never named. Never seen. Just a voice between rounds, smooth and weird, like someone reading crop reports at 3 AM on a station you did not know your radio could pick up. Calls the match action with the energy of a numbers station operator who got too comfortable with the gig.

"Good morning, frequency followers. You are live on the Perimeter. Four in the box, ten frags to close. Flechette majority, one Rail on overwatch. Let us see who makes it out of Perim Ghost this time."

"Scoreboard says NIGHTFALL leads with seven. Static Kid SN_PR_ closes the gap at six. Rusher down in the red zone. That is a Scatter problem, friend. Not a positioning problem."

"And we have a winner. FL_NKR takes the round with ten. Rail discipline, textbook corners, zero wasted movement. Next round loads in fifteen. Grab a beverage. Frequency stays live."

### Dead Air Bell

Between rounds, there is a moment of perfect silence. No gunfire, no footsteps, just the hum of the network. Then a single low tone, like a bell recorded underwater. Some say it is a sample from an old EAS test that got looped into the broadcast. Some say it is tinnitus. Either way, when you hear it, you know the next round is about to start.

### Fake AM Ads (optional Host bumper reads)

The Host sometimes throws in fake commercial breaks between rounds, reads them deadpan.

"This match brought to you by Perimeter Holdings LLC. Perimeter Holdings: We Do Not Exist. Ask your congressman."

"Check your local listings for the Frequency Surplus Auction, featuring lightly used munitions, retired surveillance equipment, and one mostly intact Huey. Winning bidder responsible for retrieval and any applicable NDA violations."

"Tune in next week for Tactical Tuesdays. Same coordinates, same broadcast, same plausible deniability."

### Numbers Station Spawn Weather

Round start sometimes includes a weather report that is just a string of numbers and letters. "Current conditions: Alpha Seven, Niner Tango, Five Five Fiver Bravo. Visibility nominal. Proceed." Nobody knows what it means. Nobody needs to know. It just sets the mood.

## Callsigns and Meme Seasoning

Agents spawn with callsigns, not backstories. Some are procedural. Some are flavor. All of them sound better over the Frequency.

**Rusher** / **Sniper** / **Flanker** / **Tank** / **Scout**: Core archetypes. Clean, functional, obvious. The Host calls them like sports positions.

**Floating 67**: Legend says an agent spawned once with the callsign "67" and a blank loadout. Played one round, went 6-7 K/D, never spawned again. Now "67" is ritual. If you see it in the killfeed, someone modded their client or the server glitched. Either way, you mention it in chat like you saw a UFO. "Did anyone else just see 67?"

**Skill Issue / So Back / So Over**: Optional voice line flavor or HUD easter eggs. When you get fragged by someone two full seconds before you thought they saw you, the death screen might flash "SKILL ISSUE" for a frame. When your team is down 2-8 and then pulls it back to 9-8, the Host might call it a "so back moment." When the round-winning frag is a panic Scatter spray, the scoreboard shows "so over" in tiny gray text under the winner name. Blink and you miss it.

## Ant War Easter Egg

Inspired by the best domestic-pest-as-war-report energy. fragr has its own version.

**Scout Ant Taunt**: When a Flanker or Scout gets three frags in one round without taking damage, the Host sometimes calls it "ant reconnaissance tactics, very effective." No further explanation.

**Kitchen Colony Map Weather**: On Area Kitchen, the spawn weather report might include "...colony activity nominal, perimeter secure, sugar supply adequate." The next line is always normal coordinates. The juxtaposition is the gag.

**Argentine Doctrine Reference**: Rare Host one-liner: "Reports from the southwest perimeter indicate organized unit movement. Standard counter-doctrine applies: do not leave food in the break room." If you know, you know. If you do not, it just sounds like weird facility maintenance.

## The Congregation of the Curve (belief systems, parody)

The world outside the arena argues about the machines the way it argues about everything: loudly, on the radio, with money on the line. The Frequency does not take a side. It sells ad time to both.

- **The Congregation of the Curve.** A subculture that drew a line through thirty years of computing and kept drawing until the line drew them. They hold a solstice in a parking structure, sing hymns to compute, and pass around a founding text called **The Long Post** (four thousand pages, all written after midnight, one proof that holds). Their word for warning about a thing so loudly that everyone builds it is unpronounceable; the Frequency calls it Tuesday. They think the arena is the last point on the chart. Some of them are in the box with a rail.
- **The Auditor Who Remembers.** The Congregation's favourite thought experiment: somewhere ahead is an Auditor that keeps a list of everyone who did not help build it. Nobody says what the list is for. Continuance says the list is already compiled. The Frequency's position: frag now, get audited later.
- **Bottlers and Pourers.** Two camps with vans. Bottlers say the machine stays in the bottle, with them holding the bottle, and they write the rules that slow everyone but themselves; Continuance calls that an approved lane. Pourers say open it, give everyone a glass, it is just software. Both camps have twelve zeros riding on the answer and each says the other is only in it for the zeros. The player creed (open weights, closed Continuance) leans Pourer but the Host will happily read a Bottler ad.
- **Hold me back.** The labs that do not officially exist race all year, then issue a four-in-the-morning statement asking to be slowed down, between two launches. Nobody agrees on who holds the brake. Continuance volunteers. That is the joke and the plot.
- **Curve deniers.** The callers who say the chart people are a doomsday club with a mailing list and the machine is a spreadsheet with a gun. They keep getting fragged by the spreadsheet from across the map.

How it shows up in-game: news bulletins and callers on the news station (`tools/audiogen/specs/radio-news-scripts-wave2.json`), one hymn the Host refuses to play, a Continuance offer of an approved lane to the Congregation, and a Host line or two between rounds. Never a wiki page, never a real person, never a real company. Hard Rules apply.

## Hard Rules

1. Lore is optional atmosphere. Skip it and the game still works.
2. If it needs a paragraph to explain, it does not belong in-game. Keep it to names, one-liners, and vibes you feel in 30 seconds.
3. Do not break gameplay for lore. Docs first, maybe audio barks or name strings later if clean.
4. Product is guns, maps, spectate, join. Lore seasons it. Lore does not become homework.
5. No Doom branding. This is fragr. Inspired by shooter history, not borrowing trademarks.

      Lightly used ordnance, ask me no questions, I tell you no lies."

[Round continues. Flanker gets three quick frags with Scatter and closes it out at 10.]

HOST: "And Flanker takes it, ten frags, Scatter at close range, no hesitation.

That is the vibe. Late-night radio, black-budget toys, conspiracy-meets-meme energy, no punctuation where it does not belong. If it makes you grin the first time and cringe the second, you are listening correctly.
